Why our own totals are smaller — and not in conflict

WHO counts every road in Thailand and adjusts for under-reporting. This project analyses the 110,434 highway crashes and 13,901 deaths recorded by the Ministry of Transport between 2021 and 2025 — a recorded subset of the national toll, not a contradiction of it.

Source: World Health Organization, Global status report on road safety 2023 — Thailand country profile, data year 2021. This is the only figure in the deck that does not come from Datasource/.

The limitation, stated before anyone has to ask

This file covers about 17% of Thailand's road deaths — 2,780 deaths a year here against 17,447 nationally (Department of Disease Control three-database system, 2024). It records highways, rural highways and expressways only — not city streets or local roads, which is where most motorcyclists die.

Method: risk is measured against exposure — crashes per vehicle-kilometre, using traffic volume × section length × years — so a busy road is not automatically a dangerous one. Every conclusion that follows is about highways, not about Thailand as a whole: motorcycles are 36% of deaths in this file against roughly 81% nationally.

Part 1 · Descriptive

Alcohol is in the deadliest tier of crash causes

Speeding is 71% of crashes. Scroll to see the same 18 causes reordered by how often they kill instead of how often they happen.

Alcohol is 1.4% of recorded crashes but kills 22.0 per 100 — against 12.6 across all crashes and 11.7 for speeding. Two rarer causes score higher on 400–500 crashes each; alcohol's rate rests on 1,565 — so the point is the magnitude, not the ranking.

Part 1 · Descriptive

Time of day decides deployment; day of week does not

Counted by deaths the peak moves to 19:00 and the spread widens to 2.7×. The weekday panel barely moves — 13.4–15.1%, Friday highest on either count — so the day of the week still does not decide anything. Alcohol is why the evening does: 53% of alcohol-related crashes fall on Friday–Sunday and 59% between 19:00 and 04:00. A checkpoint schedule built on average daily traffic alone would miss this window entirely.

Part 1 · Descriptive

Motorcycles are a seventh of crashes and over a third of deaths

Pickups are 37% of crashes and kill 9.0 per 100. Motorcycles are 14% of crashes and kill 32.0 — 3.6× more per crash. Scroll to count deaths instead of crashes, then to turn the carousel through road alignment and weather.

Counted by deaths the order inverts: motorcycles are 36% of all highway deaths, ahead of pickups at 27%. Road alignment barely moves — straight road is 71.5% of crashes and 70.0% of deaths, at 12.3 per 100, no safer than a curve. Weather moves the other way: rain is 12% of crashes but only 8% of deaths, and clear weather is deadlier per crash — 13.1 against 7.9.

A motorcycle is involved in crashes accounting for 55.6% of deaths — the largest single fact in this file. It is not drawn as a bar because involvement overlaps: 31.5% of crashes involve more than one vehicle type and the per-type shares sum to 159%, so they are not slices of a whole. The chart counts each crash once, by first vehicle recorded.
